Below I repair the main gaps in the previous outline and then give the full proof.

The two important repairs are:

  1. CM tower gap. An arbitrary unramified tower over a CM field need not have all layers CM. I avoid this by first constructing a totally real \(T\)-split unramified tower \(F_i\), and then setting

    \[ K_i=F_i(i). \]

    Then \(K_i\) is automatically CM.

  2. Class-number bound gap. The previous proof invoked an analytic class-number estimate. We can instead use a completely elementary exponential class-number bound from Minkowski’s theorem plus \(\zeta_K(2)\le \zeta(2)^{[K:\mathbb Q]}\).

We will use one standard arithmetic input: the Golod–Shafarevich criterion for \(T\)-split class field towers.

Proof

We identify \(\mathbb C\) with \(\mathbb R^2\).

1. A bounded-root-discriminant CM family with one completely split prime

Fix \(\ell=5\).

Choose \(20\) distinct rational primes

\[ r_1,\dots,r_{20}\equiv 1 \pmod {20}, \]

and put

\[ D=r_1\cdots r_{20}. \]

Then \(D\equiv 1\pmod 4\), so

\[ F_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D) \]

is a real quadratic field of discriminant \(D\). Also \(D\equiv 1\pmod 5\), so \(5\) splits in \(F_0\). Let \(T\) be the set of the two primes of \(F_0\) above \(5\).

By genus theory,

\[ d_2\operatorname{Cl}^+(F_0)=19, \]

where \(\operatorname{Cl}^+(F_0)\) is the narrow class group and

\[ d_2 A:=\dim_{\mathbb F_2} A/A^2. \]

Quotienting by the classes of the two primes in \(T\) lowers the \(2\)-rank by at most \(2\), so the \(T\)-split narrow \(2\)-class group has \(2\)-rank at least

\[ 19-2=17. \]

Now use the Golod–Shafarevich–Shafarevich theorem in the following standard form.

If \(F\) is a number field, \(T\) a finite set of finite primes, and \(G\) is the Galois group of the maximal pro-\(2\) extension of \(F\) unramified at all finite primes and split completely at all real places and at all primes of \(T\), then \(G\) has generator rank

\[ s=d_2\bigl(\operatorname{Cl}^+(F)/\langle T\rangle\bigr) \]

and relation rank at most

\[ s+r_1(F)+r_2(F)+|T|+1. \]

If this upper bound is \(<s^2/4\), then \(G\) is infinite.

For \(F_0\), we have \(r_1=2\), \(r_2=0\), \(|T|=2\), and \(s\ge 17\). Hence the relation rank is at most

\[ s+5, \]

while

\[ s+5<\frac{s^2}{4} \]

for all \(s\ge 17\). Therefore the \(T\)-split narrow \(2\)-class field tower of \(F_0\) is infinite.

Let

\[ F_1,F_2,\dots \]

be finite layers in this tower with

\[ d_i:=[F_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty. \]

Then:

  • every \(F_i\) is totally real;
  • \(F_i/F_0\) is unramified at all finite primes;
  • every prime of \(T\) splits completely in \(F_i\);
  • therefore \(5\) splits completely in \(F_i\);
  • and
\[ \operatorname{rd}(F_i)=\operatorname{rd}(F_0)=\sqrt D. \]

Now define

\[ K_i:=F_i(i). \]

Since \(F_i\) is totally real, \(K_i\) is CM, with maximal totally real subfield \(F_i\). Also \(5\) splits in \(\mathbb Q(i)\), because \(5\equiv 1\pmod 4\). Hence \(5\) splits completely in \(K_i\).

Moreover,

\[ [K_i:\mathbb Q]=2d_i. \]

The relative discriminant of \(F_i(i)/F_i\) divides \(4\mathcal O_{F_i}\), so

\[ \operatorname{rd}(K_i)\le 2\operatorname{rd}(F_i)=2\sqrt D. \]

Set

\[ R_0:=2\sqrt D. \]

Thus we have an infinite sequence of CM fields \(K_i\) with

\[ [K_i:\mathbb Q]=2d_i,\qquad d_i\to\infty, \]

such that

\[ \operatorname{rd}(K_i)\le R_0 \]

and \(5\) splits completely in every \(K_i\).

2. A uniform exponential class-number bound

We need the following elementary estimate.

Lemma

There is a constant \(H\), depending only on \(R_0\), such that for every \(K_i\),

\[ h_{K_i}\le H^{d_i}. \]

Proof

Let \(K\) be one of the \(K_i\), and write

\[ [K:\mathbb Q]=2d. \]

Since \(K\) is totally imaginary, Minkowski’s bound says that every ideal class contains an integral ideal \(\mathfrak a\) with

\[ N\mathfrak a\le \left(\frac{4}{\pi}\right)^d \frac{(2d)!}{(2d)^{2d}} \sqrt{|\Delta_K|}. \]

Using \((2d)!\le (2d)^{2d}\) and

\[ \sqrt{|\Delta_K|}\le R_0^d, \]

we get

\[ N\mathfrak a\le \left(\frac{4R_0}{\pi}\right)^d. \]

Let

\[ A:=\frac{4R_0}{\pi}. \]

Then every ideal class has a representative of norm at most \(A^d\).

Let \(I_K(X)\) be the number of integral ideals of \(K\) with norm at most \(X\). For \(X\ge 1\),

\[ I_K(X) \le X^2 \zeta_K(2). \]

Also,

\[ \zeta_K(2)\le \zeta(2)^{2d}. \]

Indeed, each Euler factor of \(\zeta_K(2)\) is bounded by the corresponding Euler factor of \(\zeta(2)^{2d}\).

Therefore

\[ h_K\le I_K(A^d) \le A^{2d}\zeta(2)^{2d}. \]

So it is enough to take

\[ H:=A^2\zeta(2)^2 = \left(\frac{4R_0}{\pi}\right)^2\zeta(2)^2. \]

This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

3. Choice of parameters

For a positive integer \(m\), define

\[ C_m:=(1+2\cdot 5^m)^2 \]

and

\[ L_m:=\frac{(m+1)\pi 5^{2m}}{2HR_0}. \]

We choose \(m\) so large that

\[ L_m>C_m. \]

This is possible because

\[ \frac{L_m}{C_m} = \frac{(m+1)\pi 5^{2m}} {2HR_0(1+2\cdot 5^m)^2} \sim \frac{\pi(m+1)}{8HR_0} \to\infty. \]

Now fix such an \(m\), and define

\[ \delta := \frac12\left(\frac{\log L_m}{\log C_m}-1\right)>0. \]

Equivalently,

\[ L_m=C_m^{1+2\delta}. \]

This \(\delta\) is an absolute constant.

4. Many unit translations inside a fractional ideal

Fix one of the fields \(K=K_i\), and write

\[ [K:\mathbb Q]=2d. \]

Let

\[ F=F_i \]

be its maximal totally real subfield.

Choose the \(d\) embeddings

\[ \sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_d:K\hookrightarrow \mathbb C \]

corresponding to the \(d\) real embeddings of \(F\) and the choice \(i\mapsto i\). Thus

\[ \sigma_r(\overline x)=\overline{\sigma_r(x)} \]

for CM conjugation \(x\mapsto \overline x\).

Because \(5\) splits completely in \(K\), we may write

\[ 5\mathcal O_K = \prod_{j=1}^d \mathfrak q_j\overline{\mathfrak q_j}, \]

where each \(\mathfrak q_j\) has norm \(5\), and \(\overline{\mathfrak q_j}\) is its CM conjugate.

Let

\[ \mathcal E:=\{0,1,\dots,m\}^d. \]

For

\[ \mathbf e=(e_1,\dots,e_d)\in\mathcal E, \]

define

\[ \mathfrak Q_{\mathbf e}:=\prod_{j=1}^d \mathfrak q_j^{e_j}. \]

The map

\[ \mathbf e\mapsto [\mathfrak Q_{\mathbf e}]\in\operatorname{Cl}(K) \]

takes \((m+1)^d\) values into a class group of size at most \(H^d\). Hence some fiber \(\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal E\) has size at least

\[ |\mathcal F|\ge \left(\frac{m+1}{H}\right)^d. \]

Fix \(\mathbf e_*\in\mathcal F\).

For every \(\mathbf e\in\mathcal F\), the fractional ideal

\[ \mathfrak Q_{\mathbf e}\mathfrak Q_{\mathbf e_*}^{-1} \]

is principal. Choose \(z_{\mathbf e}\in K^\times\) such that

\[ (z_{\mathbf e}) = \mathfrak Q_{\mathbf e}\mathfrak Q_{\mathbf e_*}^{-1}. \]

Define

\[ \theta_{\mathbf e}:= \frac{z_{\mathbf e}}{\overline{z_{\mathbf e}}}. \]

Then

\[ \theta_{\mathbf e}\overline{\theta_{\mathbf e}}=1. \]

Therefore, for every \(r\),

\[ |\sigma_r(\theta_{\mathbf e})|=1. \]

In particular,

\[ |\sigma_1(\theta_{\mathbf e})|=1. \]

Also,

\[ (\theta_{\mathbf e}) = \prod_{j=1}^d \mathfrak q_j^{e_j-e_{*,j}} \overline{\mathfrak q_j}^{-(e_j-e_{*,j})}. \]

Since each exponent \(e_j-e_{*,j}\) lies between \(-m\) and \(m\), all finite valuations of \(\theta_{\mathbf e}\) are at least \(-m\) at primes above \(5\), and are nonnegative elsewhere. Hence

\[ \theta_{\mathbf e}\in 5^{-m}\mathcal O_K. \]

Finally, the \(\theta_{\mathbf e}\) are distinct: the valuation of \((\theta_{\mathbf e})\) at \(\mathfrak q_j\) is exactly \(e_j-e_{*,j}\), so the ideal \((\theta_{\mathbf e})\) determines \(\mathbf e\).

Thus the set

\[ \Theta:=\{\theta_{\mathbf e}:\mathbf e\in\mathcal F\} \]

satisfies

\[ |\Theta|\ge \left(\frac{m+1}{H}\right)^d, \]

and every \(\theta\in\Theta\) lies in \(5^{-m}\mathcal O_K\) and has

\[ |\sigma_1(\theta)|=1. \]

5. The model set

Let

\[ \Lambda:=\sigma(5^{-m}\mathcal O_K)\subset \mathbb C^d, \]

where

\[ \sigma(\alpha):=(\sigma_1(\alpha),\dots,\sigma_d(\alpha)). \]

This is a full lattice in \(\mathbb C^d\simeq\mathbb R^{2d}\).

Let

\[ B_d:=\{x\in\mathbb C^d:\|x\|_2\le \sqrt d\}. \]

For \(y\in\mathbb C^d\), define

\[ X_y:= \{\alpha\in 5^{-m}\mathcal O_K:\sigma(\alpha)\in y+B_d\}, \]

and define the planar point set

\[ P_y:=\{\sigma_1(\alpha):\alpha\in X_y\}\subset\mathbb C\simeq\mathbb R^2. \]

Since \(\sigma_1\) is an embedding, it is injective, so

\[ |P_y|=|X_y|. \]

6. Uniform upper bound for \(|P_y|\)

We show that for every \(y\),

\[ |P_y|\le C_m^d. \]

Let \(\alpha,\beta\in 5^{-m}\mathcal O_K\), \(\alpha\ne\beta\). Put

\[ \gamma:=\alpha-\beta. \]

Then

\[ 5^m\gamma\in\mathcal O_K\setminus\{0\}. \]

Therefore

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(5^m\gamma)|\ge 1. \]

Since \([K:\mathbb Q]=2d\),

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\gamma)|\ge 5^{-2md}. \]

But

\[ |N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\gamma)| = \prod_{r=1}^d |\sigma_r(\gamma)|^2. \]

By AM-GM,

\[ \|\sigma(\gamma)\|_2^2 = \sum_{r=1}^d |\sigma_r(\gamma)|^2 \ge d\left(\prod_{r=1}^d |\sigma_r(\gamma)|^2\right)^{1/d} \ge d5^{-2m}. \]

Hence distinct points of \(\Lambda\) are separated by at least

\[ \sqrt d\,5^{-m}. \]

All points of \(\Lambda\cap(y+B_d)\) lie in a ball of radius \(\sqrt d\). Packing balls of radius

\[ \frac12\sqrt d\,5^{-m} \]

around them gives

\[ |X_y| \le \left(1+2\cdot 5^m\right)^{2d} = C_m^d. \]

Thus

\[ |P_y|\le C_m^d. \]

7. Averaging unit-translation incidences

For \(\theta\in\Theta\), define

\[ M_\theta(y) := \left| \left\{ \alpha\in X_y:\alpha+\theta\in X_y \right\} \right|. \]

Each such \(\alpha\) gives a unit-distance pair in \(P_y\), since

\[ |\sigma_1(\alpha+\theta)-\sigma_1(\alpha)| = |\sigma_1(\theta)| = 1. \]

Let

\[ I(y):=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}M_\theta(y). \]

We will average \(I(y)\) over the torus \(\mathbb C^d/\Lambda\).

First compute the covolume of \(\Lambda\). The usual Minkowski covolume formula gives

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\sigma(\mathcal O_K)) = 2^{-d}\sqrt{|\Delta_K|}. \]

Therefore

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Lambda) = 5^{-2md}2^{-d}\sqrt{|\Delta_K|} \le 5^{-2md}2^{-d}R_0^d. \]

Also

\[ \operatorname{vol}(B_d) = \frac{\pi^d d^d}{d!} \ge \pi^d. \]

Hence

\[ \frac{\operatorname{vol}(B_d)}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)} \ge \left(\frac{2\pi 5^{2m}}{R_0}\right)^d. \]

Now fix \(\theta\in\Theta\). Put

\[ v:=\sigma(\theta)\in\Lambda. \]

Since \(|\sigma_r(\theta)|=1\) for every \(r\),

\[ \|v\|_2=\sqrt d. \]

The intersection

\[ B_d\cap(B_d-v) \]

contains a ball of radius \(\sqrt d/2\). Therefore

\[ \operatorname{vol}\bigl(B_d\cap(B_d-v)\bigr) \ge 4^{-d}\operatorname{vol}(B_d). \]

For any bounded measurable set \(S\subset\mathbb C^d\), the standard lattice averaging identity gives

\[ \mathbb E_{y\in\mathbb C^d/\Lambda} |\Lambda\cap(y+S)| = \frac{\operatorname{vol}(S)}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}. \]

Applying this with

\[ S=B_d\cap(B_d-v) \]

gives

\[ \mathbb E_y M_\theta(y) \ge 4^{-d} \frac{\operatorname{vol}(B_d)}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)} \ge 4^{-d} \left(\frac{2\pi 5^{2m}}{R_0}\right)^d. \]

Summing over \(\theta\in\Theta\), we obtain

\[ \mathbb E_y I(y) \ge \left(\frac{m+1}{H}\right)^d 4^{-d} \left(\frac{2\pi 5^{2m}}{R_0}\right)^d. \]

Thus

\[ \mathbb E_y I(y) \ge \left( \frac{(m+1)\pi 5^{2m}}{2HR_0} \right)^d = L_m^d. \]

Therefore there exists some \(y\) such that

\[ I(y)\ge L_m^d. \]

Fix such a \(y\), and set

\[ P:=P_y. \]

8. Unit-distance count

The quantity \(I(y)\) counts distinct directed unit-distance pairs in \(P\). Indeed, if

\[ (\alpha,\theta) \]

contributes to \(I(y)\), then

\[ \sigma_1(\alpha),\ \sigma_1(\alpha+\theta)\in P \]

and their distance is \(1\). Moreover, the map

\[ (\alpha,\theta)\mapsto \bigl(\sigma_1(\alpha),\sigma_1(\alpha+\theta)\bigr) \]

is injective because \(\sigma_1\) is injective on \(K\).

Thus the number of unordered unit-distance pairs satisfies

\[ u(P)\ge \frac12 I(y)\ge \frac12 L_m^d. \]

On the other hand,

\[ |P|\le C_m^d. \]

Since

\[ L_m=C_m^{1+2\delta}, \]

we have

\[ u(P) \ge \frac12 C_m^{(1+2\delta)d}. \]

For all sufficiently large \(d\),

\[ \frac12 C_m^{(1+2\delta)d} \ge C_m^{(1+\delta)d}. \]

Therefore, for all sufficiently large \(d\),

\[ u(P)\ge C_m^{(1+\delta)d}\ge |P|^{1+\delta}. \]

9. Forming the infinite sequence

Apply the construction to the fields \(K_i\). Since

\[ d_i\to\infty, \]

we may discard finitely many initial fields so that the final inequality holds for every remaining \(i\). Relabel the remaining point sets as

\[ P_1,P_2,\dots. \]

Then

\[ u(P_i)\ge |P_i|^{1+\delta} \]

for every \(i\).

Finally, \(|P_i|\to\infty\). Indeed,

\[ u(P_i)\ge \frac12 L_m^{d_i}\to\infty, \]

and always

\[ u(P_i)\le \binom{|P_i|}{2}. \]

Hence \(|P_i|\to\infty\).

Thus there exist an absolute constant \(\delta>0\) and an infinite sequence of finite point sets \(P_i\subset\mathbb R^2\) with \(|P_i|\to\infty\) and

\[ u(P_i)\ge |P_i|^{1+\delta}. \]

This proves the theorem. \(\square\)
