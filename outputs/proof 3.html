Below I first point out the main gaps in the earlier sketch, then give a repaired proof.

The previous argument had three places needing real justification:

  1. The split CM tower was asserted too quickly. I replace it with a concrete Golod-Shafarevich construction: first build a totally real infinite unramified \(3\)-class tower in which \(5\) splits completely, then adjoin \(i\).

  2. The lattice-counting estimate was quoted without proof. I state and justify the needed van der Corput/Blichfeldt estimate.

  3. The class-number and double-counting issues need explicit constants. I give uniform class-number bounds, choose parameters carefully, and account for the factor \(1/2\) in the definition of \(u(P)\).

We identify \(\mathbb R^2\) with \(\mathbb C\).

Proof

1. A totally real tower in which \(5\) splits completely

We use a standard Golod-Shafarevich input.

Standard class-field-tower criterion

Let \(F\) be a number field, let \(\ell\) be an odd prime, and let \(T\) be a finite set of finite primes of \(F\). Let \(G_T(F,\ell)\) be the Galois group of the maximal everywhere finite-unramified pro-\(\ell\) extension of \(F\) in which all primes of \(T\) split completely.

Let \(d\) and \(r\) be the generator and relation ranks of \(G_T(F,\ell)\). Shafarevich’s relation-rank estimate gives

\[ r\le d+r_1(F)+r_2(F)+|T|+1. \]

If \(G_T(F,\ell)\) is finite, then the Golod-Shafarevich inequality gives

\[ r>\frac{d^2}{4}. \]

We will use this with \(\ell=3\).

1.1. Construction of the base field

Choose an integer \(t\) large enough that

\[ \frac{(t-1)^2}{4}>(t-1)+7. \]

For example, \(t=20\) works.

By Chebotarev, choose distinct rational primes

\[ s_1,\dots,s_t \]

which split completely in the splitting field of \(x^3-5\). Then each \(s_a\) satisfies

\[ s_a\equiv 1\pmod 3 \]

and \(5\) is a cube modulo \(s_a\).

For each \(a\), let \(F_a\) be the unique cyclic cubic subfield of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{s_a})\). Since \(s_a\equiv 1\pmod 3\), such a field exists. It is totally real. Also, since \(5\) is a cube modulo \(s_a\), the Frobenius of \(5\) in \(F_a/\mathbb Q\) is trivial, so \(5\) splits completely in \(F_a\).

Let

\[ E:=F_1F_2\cdots F_t. \]

The fields \(F_a\) are linearly disjoint because their conductors are pairwise coprime, so

\[ \operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\cong (\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z)^t. \]

Choose an isomorphism

\[ \operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)\cong \mathbb F_3^t \]

and define a surjection

\[ \varphi:\mathbb F_3^t\to \mathbb F_3,\qquad (x_1,\dots,x_t)\mapsto x_1+\cdots+x_t. \]

Let

\[ F:=E^{\ker\varphi}. \]

Then \(F/\mathbb Q\) is cyclic cubic and totally real.

Each prime \(s_a\) ramifies in \(F\): indeed, the inertia subgroup at \(s_a\) in \(E/\mathbb Q\) is the \(a\)-th coordinate copy of \(\mathbb F_3\), and \(\varphi\) is nontrivial on it.

Moreover, \(E/F\) is everywhere unramified at finite primes. At each \(s_a\), the inertia subgroup in \(E/\mathbb Q\) maps injectively to \(\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\), so the relative inertia in \(E/F\) is trivial. At all other finite primes there is no ramification in \(E/\mathbb Q\).

Since \(5\) splits completely in every \(F_a\), it splits completely in \(E\), hence also in \(F\). Let \(T\) be the set of the three primes of \(F\) above \(5\).

Now \(E/F\) is an everywhere unramified elementary abelian \(3\)-extension of degree

\[ [E:F]=3^{t-1}, \]

and all primes of \(T\) split completely in \(E/F\). Hence \(G_T(F,3)\) has an elementary abelian quotient of rank \(t-1\), so its generator rank satisfies

\[ d\ge t-1. \]

Since \(F\) is totally real cubic,

\[ r_1(F)=3,\qquad r_2(F)=0,\qquad |T|=3. \]

Thus Shafarevich gives

\[ r\le d+7. \]

If \(G_T(F,3)\) were finite, Golod-Shafarevich would imply

\[ r>\frac{d^2}{4}. \]

But \(d\ge t-1\), and \(t\) was chosen so that

\[ \frac{d^2}{4}>d+7. \]

This is impossible. Therefore \(G_T(F,3)\) is infinite.

Choose finite Galois subextensions

\[ F\subset K_1\subset K_2\subset\cdots \]

inside this infinite pro-\(3\) extension with

\[ [K_j:F]\to\infty. \]

Each \(K_j/F\) is unramified at all finite primes, all primes above \(5\) split completely, and \(K_j\) is totally real because \(K_j/F\) has odd degree and is Galois.

Therefore

\[ [K_j:\mathbb Q]\to\infty, \]

the rational prime \(5\) splits completely in every \(K_j\), and

\[ \operatorname{rd}(K_j)=\operatorname{rd}(F) \]

is constant.

Set

\[ \kappa:=\operatorname{rd}(F). \]

2. Passing to CM fields

Define

\[ L_j:=K_j(i). \]

Since \(K_j\) is totally real, \(L_j\) is a CM field. Let

\[ \tau_j:L_j\to L_j \]

be complex conjugation over \(K_j\), so \(\tau_j(i)=-i\).

Write

\[ r_j:=[K_j:\mathbb Q]. \]

Then

\[ [L_j:\mathbb Q]=2r_j, \qquad r_j\to\infty. \]

Because \(5\) splits completely in \(K_j\), and because \(5\equiv 1\pmod 4\), the polynomial \(x^2+1\) splits modulo every prime of \(K_j\) above \(5\). Hence \(5\) splits completely in \(L_j\). Thus

\[ 5\mathcal O_{L_j} = \prod_{a=1}^{r_j} \mathfrak p_a\,\tau_j(\mathfrak p_a), \]

where the \(2r_j\) primes above \(5\) are paired by \(\tau_j\).

The root discriminants of \(L_j\) are uniformly bounded. Indeed, the relative discriminant of \(K_j(i)/K_j\) divides \(4\mathcal O_{K_j}\), so

\[ |\Delta_{L_j}| \le |\Delta_{K_j}|^2\,4^{r_j}. \]

Taking \(1/(2r_j)\)-th powers gives

\[ \operatorname{rd}(L_j)\le 2\operatorname{rd}(K_j)=2\kappa. \]

Set

\[ \rho:=2\kappa. \]

Thus

\[ \operatorname{rd}(L_j)\le \rho \]

for every \(j\).

3. Uniform estimates for CM fields of bounded root discriminant

Let \(L\) be one of the \(L_j\), with

\[ [L:\mathbb Q]=2r. \]

Choose one embedding from each conjugate pair,

\[ \Sigma=\{\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_r\}. \]

For \(S>0\), define

\[ \mathcal C_L(S) := \left\{ \alpha\in\mathcal O_L: |\sigma(\alpha)|\le S \text{ for all }\sigma\in\Sigma \right\}. \]

Because \(L\) is CM, this is equivalent to bounding all complex conjugates.

3.1. Lattice count

The Minkowski embedding

\[ \Phi:\mathcal O_L\hookrightarrow \mathbb C^r \]

has covolume

\[ 2^{-r}\sqrt{|\Delta_L|}. \]

The body

\[ \{z\in\mathbb C^r: |z_a|\le S\text{ for all }a\} \]

has volume

\[ (\pi S^2)^r. \]

By the van der Corput lattice-counting inequality,

\[ |\mathcal C_L(S)| \ge \frac{(\pi S^2)^r}{2^{2r}\cdot 2^{-r}\sqrt{|\Delta_L|}} = \left(\frac{\pi S^2}{2\operatorname{rd}(L)}\right)^r. \]

Since \(\operatorname{rd}(L)\le \rho\),

\[ |\mathcal C_L(S)| \ge \left(\frac{\pi S^2}{2\rho}\right)^r. \]

Also,

\[ |\mathcal C_L(S)|\le (2S+1)^{2r}. \]

Indeed, if \(k=\lfloor 2S\rfloor+1\), reduction modulo \(k\mathcal O_L\) is injective on \(\mathcal C_L(S)\). If \(\alpha\ne \beta\) and \(\alpha\equiv\beta\pmod{k\mathcal O_L}\), then \(\gamma=\alpha-\beta=k\eta\) for some nonzero \(\eta\in\mathcal O_L\), so

\[ |N_{L/\mathbb Q}(\gamma)|\ge k^{2r}. \]

But

\[ |\sigma(\gamma)|\le 2S \]

for every embedding, so

\[ |N_{L/\mathbb Q}(\gamma)|\le (2S)^{2r}<k^{2r}, \]

a contradiction.

Thus

\[ |\mathcal C_L(S)|\le |\mathcal O_L/k\mathcal O_L| = k^{2r} \le (2S+1)^{2r}. \]

3.2. Class-number bound

There is a constant \(H\), depending only on \(\rho\), such that

\[ h_L\le H^r \]

for every \(L=L_j\).

Indeed, Minkowski’s ideal theorem gives an integral ideal of norm at most

\[ \left(\frac{4}{\pi}\right)^r \frac{(2r)!}{(2r)^{2r}} \sqrt{|\Delta_L|} \le \left(\frac{4\rho}{\pi}\right)^r \]

in every ideal class.

The number of ideals of norm at most \(X\) is at most \(X^2\zeta_L(2)\). Also,

\[ \zeta_L(2)\le \zeta(2)^{2r}. \]

Therefore

\[ h_L \le \left(\frac{4\rho}{\pi}\right)^{2r} \zeta(2)^{2r}. \]

So it suffices to take

\[ H:= \max\left\{ 2,\left(\frac{4\rho\,\zeta(2)}{\pi}\right)^2 \right\}. \]

4. Many norm-one \(S\)-units of bounded denominator

Fix \(L=L_j\), write \(r=[L:\mathbb Q]/2\), and write \(\tau=\tau_j\).

Since \(5\) splits completely,

\[ 5\mathcal O_L = \prod_{a=1}^r \mathfrak p_a\,\tau(\mathfrak p_a). \]

Choose an integer \(m\ge 1\), to be fixed later. For

\[ e=(e_1,\dots,e_r)\in\{0,1,\dots,m\}^r, \]

define

\[ I_e:=\prod_{a=1}^r \mathfrak p_a^{e_a}. \]

There are \((m+1)^r\) such ideals. Since \(h_L\le H^r\), some ideal class contains at least

\[ \left(\frac{m+1}{H}\right)^r \]

of them. Let \(E\subseteq\{0,\dots,m\}^r\) be such a set. Fix \(e^{(0)}\in E\).

For every \(e\in E\), the fractional ideal

\[ I_eI_{e^{(0)}}^{-1} \]

is principal. Choose \(a_e\in L^\times\) with

\[ (a_e)=I_eI_{e^{(0)}}^{-1}. \]

Define

\[ w_e:=\frac{a_e}{\tau(a_e)}. \]

Then

\[ w_e\tau(w_e)=1. \]

Therefore, for every complex embedding \(\sigma:L\hookrightarrow\mathbb C\),

\[ |\sigma(w_e)|=1. \]

Indeed, because \(\tau\) is the CM involution,

\[ \sigma(\tau x)=\overline{\sigma(x)}. \]

Thus

\[ |\sigma(w_e)|^2 = \sigma(w_e)\overline{\sigma(w_e)} = \sigma(w_e\tau(w_e)) = 1. \]

The principal ideal of \(w_e\) is

\[ (w_e) = \prod_{a=1}^r \mathfrak p_a^{e_a-e_a^{(0)}} \tau(\mathfrak p_a)^{-(e_a-e_a^{(0)})}. \]

Hence distinct \(e\)'s give distinct \(w_e\)'s.

Let

\[ W_L:=\{w_e:e\in E\}. \]

Then

\[ |W_L|\ge \left(\frac{m+1}{H}\right)^r. \]

Also, if

\[ Q:=5^m, \]

then

\[ Qw_e\in\mathcal O_L \]

for every \(e\in E\), because every negative valuation of \(w_e\) at a prime over \(5\) has size at most \(m\).

5. Choosing constants

Choose \(m\) so large that, with

\[ \mu:=\frac{m+1}{H}, \]

we have

\[ \mu>\frac{36\rho}{\pi}. \]

Also choose \(m\) large enough that

\[ \frac{\pi(3\cdot 5^m+1)^2}{2\rho}>1. \]

Set

\[ Q:=5^m, \qquad T:=2Q+1, \qquad R:=3Q+1. \]

Define

\[ a_0:=\frac{\pi T^2}{2\rho}, \qquad b_0:=(2R+1)^2. \]

Since

\[ 2R+1=6Q+3=3(2Q+1)=3T, \]

we have

\[ b_0=9T^2. \]

Therefore

\[ \frac{b_0}{a_0} = \frac{9T^2}{\pi T^2/(2\rho)} = \frac{18\rho}{\pi}. \]

Our choice of \(m\) gives

\[ \frac{a_0\mu}{b_0}>2. \]

Define

\[ \eta:= \frac{\log(a_0\mu/b_0)}{\log b_0}>0. \]

Then

\[ a_0\mu=b_0^{1+\eta}. \]

Finally set

\[ \delta:=\frac{\eta}{2}>0. \]

This \(\delta\) is an absolute constant: it depends only on the fixed tower construction and the fixed integer \(m\), not on \(j\).

6. The point sets

For \(L=L_j\), choose one complex embedding

\[ \sigma_0:L\hookrightarrow\mathbb C. \]

Define

\[ P_L := \left\{ \sigma_0\left(\frac{\alpha}{Q}\right): \alpha\in\mathcal C_L(R) \right\} \subset\mathbb C\cong\mathbb R^2. \]

Because \(\sigma_0\) is injective,

\[ |P_L|=|\mathcal C_L(R)|. \]

By the upper lattice-counting bound,

\[ |P_L| \le (2R+1)^{2r} = b_0^r. \]

By the lower bound,

\[ |\mathcal C_L(T)| \ge \left(\frac{\pi T^2}{2\rho}\right)^r = a_0^r. \]

Also, since \(\pi R^2/(2\rho)>1\), we have

\[ |P_L|\ge \left(\frac{\pi R^2}{2\rho}\right)^r\to\infty \]

as \(r=r_j\to\infty\).

7. Counting unit distances

Take

\[ \alpha\in\mathcal C_L(T), \qquad w\in W_L. \]

Since \(Qw\in\mathcal O_L\), the element

\[ \beta:=\alpha+Qw \]

lies in \(\mathcal O_L\). For every \(\sigma\in\Sigma\),

\[ |\sigma(\beta)| \le |\sigma(\alpha)|+Q|\sigma(w)| \le T+Q = R. \]

Thus

\[ \beta\in\mathcal C_L(R). \]

Therefore both

\[ x:=\sigma_0\left(\frac{\alpha}{Q}\right) \]

and

\[ y:=\sigma_0\left(\frac{\beta}{Q}\right) \]

belong to \(P_L\). Moreover,

\[ y-x = \sigma_0(w), \]

and

\[ |\sigma_0(w)|=1. \]

So \((x,y)\) is an ordered unit-distance pair.

The map

\[ (\alpha,w) \mapsto \left( \sigma_0\left(\frac{\alpha}{Q}\right), \sigma_0\left(\frac{\alpha+Qw}{Q}\right) \right) \]

is injective. Indeed, the first coordinate determines \(\alpha\), and then the difference determines \(w\).

Hence the number of ordered unit-distance pairs in \(P_L\) is at least

\[ |\mathcal C_L(T)|\,|W_L|. \]

Since \(u(P_L)\) is half the number of ordered unit-distance pairs,

\[ u(P_L) \ge \frac12 |\mathcal C_L(T)|\,|W_L|. \]

Using the bounds above,

\[ u(P_L) \ge \frac12 a_0^r\mu^r = \frac12(a_0\mu)^r = \frac12 b_0^{(1+\eta)r}. \]

Since \(\delta=\eta/2\), for all sufficiently large \(r\),

\[ \frac12 b_0^{(1+\eta)r} \ge b_0^{(1+\delta)r}. \]

Thus, for all sufficiently large \(j\),

\[ u(P_{L_j}) \ge b_0^{(1+\delta)r_j}. \]

But

\[ |P_{L_j}|\le b_0^{r_j}, \]

so

\[ u(P_{L_j}) \ge |P_{L_j}|^{1+\delta}. \]

Discard finitely many initial fields and relabel the remaining point sets as

\[ P_1,P_2,\dots. \]

Then

\[ |P_i|\to\infty \]

and

\[ u(P_i)\ge |P_i|^{1+\delta} \]

for every \(i\). This proves the theorem. \(\square\)
