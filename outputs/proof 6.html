Below I first point out the main gaps in the previous outline and then give a complete proof.

The important repairs are:

  1. Tower construction: the previous proof invoked a totally real tower with \(5\) splitting completely but did not justify it carefully. We will construct such a tower using the Golod–Shafarevich class-field-tower criterion with prescribed splitting.
  2. Class-number control: we need a uniform bound \(h(L_i)\le H^{[K_i:\mathbb Q]}\). This follows from bounded root discriminant; we prove the needed estimate.
  3. Denominator control for norm-one elements: we must check carefully that the constructed \(a,b\) lie in one fixed fractional ideal \(M^{-1}\mathcal O_K\), independent of \(i\).
  4. Counting without overcounting: we verify that the directed unit-distance pairs counted from different algebraic data are indeed distinct.
  5. Final exponent: we choose constants so that the final factor \(1/2\) causes no problem.

Proof

Let

\[ D(P)=\{(p,q)\in P\times P:\|p-q\|_2=1\} \]

be the set of directed unit-distance pairs. Then

\[ u(P)=\frac12 |D(P)|. \]

We will construct finite sets \(P_i\subset\mathbb R^2\) with \(|P_i|\to\infty\) and

\[ |D(P_i)|\ge 2|P_i|^{1+\delta}. \]

1. A totally real tower in which \(5\) splits completely

We use the following standard Golod–Shafarevich consequence.

Golod–Shafarevich splitting criterion

Let \(F\) be a number field and let \(T\) be a finite set of finite primes of \(F\). Let \(G\) be the Galois group over \(F\) of the maximal pro-\(2\) extension of \(F\) which is

  • unramified at every finite prime;
  • split completely at every real place of \(F\);
  • split completely at every prime in \(T\).

Let \(d=d(G)\) and \(r=r(G)\) be the generator and relation ranks of \(G\). Then:

  1. The maximal abelian quotient of \(G\) is obtained from the \(2\)-primary part of the ordinary class group of \(F\) by killing the ideal classes of the primes in \(T\). In particular, if

    \[ \rho_T=\dim_{\mathbb F_2} \left( \operatorname{Cl}(F)/ 2\operatorname{Cl}(F)+\langle[\mathfrak t]:\mathfrak t\in T\rangle \right), \]

    then

    \[ d\ge \rho_T. \]

  2. Shafarevich’s relation-rank estimate gives

    \[ r\le d+r_1(F)+r_2(F)+|T|+1. \]

  3. If \(G\) is finite, the Golod–Shafarevich inequality gives

    \[ r>\frac{d^2}{4}. \]

Now choose \(t=100\) distinct rational primes

\[ q_1,\dots,q_t\equiv 1\pmod{20}, \]

and set

\[ m=q_1q_2\cdots q_t,\qquad F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt m). \]

Then \(F\) is real quadratic. Since \(m\equiv 1\pmod4\), its discriminant is \(m\). Also \(m\equiv 1\pmod5\), so \(5\) splits in \(F\). Let \(T\) be the two primes of \(F\) above \(5\).

By genus theory, the narrow class group of \(F\) has \(2\)-rank \(t-1\). The ordinary class group has \(2\)-rank at least \(t-2\). Killing the two classes in \(T\) lowers the \(2\)-rank by at most \(2\), so

\[ \rho_T\ge t-4=96. \]

Thus \(d\ge 96\). Since \(F\) is real quadratic, \(r_1(F)=2\), \(r_2(F)=0\), and \(|T|=2\). Therefore

\[ r\le d+5. \]

But for every \(d\ge 96\),

\[ d+5<\frac{d^2}{4}. \]

Hence \(G\) cannot be finite. Therefore \(G\) is infinite.

Choose finite subextensions

\[ F\subset K_1,K_2,\dots\subset \mathcal K \]

with

\[ k_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty. \]

Because the tower is unramified at finite primes,

\[ \operatorname{rd}(K_i)=\operatorname{rd}(F)=:\Delta \]

for all \(i\). Because every real place splits completely, each \(K_i\) is totally real. Because the two primes over \(5\) split completely in the tower, the rational prime \(5\) splits completely in each \(K_i\).

Thus we have a sequence of totally real fields \(K_i\) such that:

\[ [K_i:\mathbb Q]=k_i\to\infty, \]
\[ \operatorname{rd}(K_i)=\Delta, \]

and

\[ 5\mathcal O_{K_i}=\mathfrak p_{i,1}\cdots \mathfrak p_{i,k_i} \]

with all \(\mathfrak p_{i,j}\) distinct of residue degree \(1\).

2. A uniform class-number bound

We need a crude exponential bound for class numbers at bounded root discriminant.

Lemma 1

For every \(R\ge 1\), there is a constant \(C_{\mathrm{cl}}(R)\) such that every number field \(M\) of degree \(n\) and root discriminant at most \(R\) satisfies

\[ h(M)\le C_{\mathrm{cl}}(R)^n. \]

One may take

\[ C_{\mathrm{cl}}(R)=4R\zeta(2)^2. \]

Proof

Let \(d_M\) be the absolute discriminant of \(M\). Minkowski’s ideal-class bound says every ideal class contains an integral ideal of norm at most

\[ \left(\frac4\pi\right)^{r_2} \frac{n!}{n^n}\sqrt{|d_M|}. \]

Since

\[ \left(\frac4\pi\right)^{r_2}\le 2^n, \qquad \frac{n!}{n^n}\le 1, \qquad |d_M|^{1/n}\le R, \]

every class contains an ideal of norm at most

\[ X=(2\sqrt R)^n. \]

Hence

\[ h(M)\le \#\{\mathfrak a\subset\mathcal O_M:N\mathfrak a\le X\}. \]

The number of ideals of norm \(m\) is at most the \((n+1)\)-fold divisor function \(d_{n+1}(m)\). Therefore

\[ h(M)\le \sum_{m\le X}d_{n+1}(m). \]

Since

\[ \sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{n+1}(m)}{m^2} = \zeta(2)^{n+1}, \]

we get

\[ \sum_{m\le X}d_{n+1}(m) \le X^2\zeta(2)^{n+1}. \]

Thus

\[ h(M)\le (2\sqrt R)^{2n}\zeta(2)^{n+1} \le (4R\zeta(2)^2)^n. \]

This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

Now set

\[ L_i=K_i(i). \]

Since \(K_i\) is totally real, \(i\notin K_i\), so

\[ [L_i:\mathbb Q]=2k_i. \]

The relative discriminant of \(K_i(i)/K_i\) divides \((4)\), because \(i\) satisfies \(X^2+1=0\). Hence

\[ \operatorname{rd}(L_i)\le 2\operatorname{rd}(K_i)=2\Delta. \]

By Lemma 1, there is a constant

\[ C_0=C_{\mathrm{cl}}(2\Delta) \]

such that

\[ h(L_i)\le C_0^{2k_i}. \]

Put

\[ H=C_0^2. \]

Then

\[ h(L_i)\le H^{k_i} \]

for all \(i\).

3. Choice of constants

Choose an integer \(E\ge 1\) so large that, with

\[ A=\frac{E+1}{H}, \]

we have

\[ A>16\Delta. \]

Also choose \(E\) large enough that

\[ \frac{4M}{\sqrt\Delta}>1, \]

where

\[ M=2\cdot 5^{2E}. \]

Define

\[ C_{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{4M}{\sqrt\Delta}, \qquad C_{\mathrm{out}}=6M+1. \]

Then \(C_{\mathrm{in}}>1\). Also,

\[ \frac{C_{\mathrm{in}}}{C_{\mathrm{out}}} = \frac{4M}{\sqrt\Delta(6M+1)} > \frac{1}{2\sqrt\Delta}, \]

because \(6M+1<8M\). Therefore

\[ \Theta := A\left(\frac{C_{\mathrm{in}}}{C_{\mathrm{out}}}\right)^2 > A\cdot \frac1{4\Delta} > 4. \]

Finally define

\[ \delta= \frac{\log\Theta}{4\log C_{\mathrm{out}}}>0. \]

This \(\delta\) is absolute.

4. Many controlled unit vectors

Fix \(i\), and write

\[ K=K_i,\qquad k=k_i,\qquad L=K(i). \]

Since \(5\) splits completely in \(K\),

\[ 5\mathcal O_K=\mathfrak p_1\cdots\mathfrak p_k. \]

Because \(5\equiv 1\pmod4\), the polynomial \(X^2+1\) splits modulo \(5\). Hence each \(\mathfrak p_j\) splits in \(L\):

\[ \mathfrak p_j\mathcal O_L=\mathfrak P_j\overline{\mathfrak P}_j, \]

where the bar denotes the nontrivial automorphism of \(L/K\).

For each vector

\[ \mathbf e=(e_1,\dots,e_k)\in\{0,1,\dots,E\}^k, \]

define the integral ideal

\[ \mathfrak A_{\mathbf e} = \prod_{j=1}^k \mathfrak P_j^{e_j} \overline{\mathfrak P}_j^{E-e_j}. \]

There are \((E+1)^k\) such ideals. Since

\[ h(L)\le H^k, \]

some ideal class contains at least

\[ \frac{(E+1)^k}{H^k}=A^k \]

of them. Let \(\mathcal E\) be a subset of \(\{0,\dots,E\}^k\) of size at least \(A^k\) such that all \(\mathfrak A_{\mathbf e}\), \(\mathbf e\in\mathcal E\), lie in the same ideal class.

Fix \(\mathbf e^{(0)}\in\mathcal E\). For each \(\mathbf e\in\mathcal E\), choose \(\alpha_{\mathbf e}\in L^\times\) such that

\[ (\alpha_{\mathbf e}) = \mathfrak A_{\mathbf e}\mathfrak A_{\mathbf e^{(0)}}^{-1}. \]

Define

\[ w_{\mathbf e} = \frac{\alpha_{\mathbf e}}{\overline{\alpha_{\mathbf e}}}. \]

Then

\[ w_{\mathbf e}\overline{w_{\mathbf e}}=1. \]

Moreover,

\[ (w_{\mathbf e}) = \prod_{j=1}^k \left( \frac{\mathfrak P_j}{\overline{\mathfrak P}_j} \right)^{2(e_j-e_j^{(0)})}. \]

Therefore every valuation of \(w_{\mathbf e}\) above \(5\) lies between \(-2E\) and \(2E\), and \(w_{\mathbf e}\) has no other nonzero valuations. Hence

\[ 5^{2E}w_{\mathbf e}\in\mathcal O_L, \qquad 5^{2E}\overline{w_{\mathbf e}}\in\mathcal O_L. \]

Write

\[ a_{\mathbf e} = \frac{w_{\mathbf e}+\overline{w_{\mathbf e}}}{2}, \qquad b_{\mathbf e} = \frac{w_{\mathbf e}-\overline{w_{\mathbf e}}}{2i}. \]

Then \(a_{\mathbf e},b_{\mathbf e}\in K\). Also,

\[ M a_{\mathbf e} = 2\cdot 5^{2E} \cdot \frac{w_{\mathbf e}+\overline{w_{\mathbf e}}}{2} = 5^{2E}(w_{\mathbf e}+\overline{w_{\mathbf e}}) \in \mathcal O_L\cap K=\mathcal O_K. \]

Similarly,

\[ M b_{\mathbf e} = 5^{2E}\frac{w_{\mathbf e}-\overline{w_{\mathbf e}}}{i} \in \mathcal O_L\cap K=\mathcal O_K, \]

since \(i\) is a unit of \(\mathcal O_L\). Thus

\[ a_{\mathbf e},b_{\mathbf e}\in M^{-1}\mathcal O_K. \]

Finally,

\[ w_{\mathbf e}=a_{\mathbf e}+ib_{\mathbf e}, \]

so

\[ 1=w_{\mathbf e}\overline{w_{\mathbf e}} = (a_{\mathbf e}+ib_{\mathbf e})(a_{\mathbf e}-ib_{\mathbf e}) = a_{\mathbf e}^2+b_{\mathbf e}^2. \]

Thus every pair \((a_{\mathbf e},b_{\mathbf e})\) satisfies

\[ a_{\mathbf e}^2+b_{\mathbf e}^2=1. \]

The pairs \((a_{\mathbf e},b_{\mathbf e})\) are distinct. Indeed, if

\[ (a_{\mathbf e},b_{\mathbf e})=(a_{\mathbf f},b_{\mathbf f}), \]

then \(w_{\mathbf e}=w_{\mathbf f}\), so \((w_{\mathbf e})=(w_{\mathbf f})\). Comparing valuations at \(\mathfrak P_j\) gives

\[ 2(e_j-e_j^{(0)})=2(f_j-e_j^{(0)}) \]

for every \(j\), hence \(\mathbf e=\mathbf f\).

Therefore we have constructed a set

\[ \mathcal U_K\subset (M^{-1}\mathcal O_K)^2 \]

such that

\[ |\mathcal U_K|\ge A^k \]

and every \((a,b)\in\mathcal U_K\) satisfies

\[ a^2+b^2=1. \]

Consequently, for every real embedding \(\tau:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R\),

\[ \tau(a)^2+\tau(b)^2=1, \]

so

\[ |\tau(a)|\le 1,\qquad |\tau(b)|\le 1. \]

5. Minkowski boxes

Let

\[ \tau_1,\dots,\tau_k:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R \]

be the real embeddings of \(K\), and let

\[ \Phi:K\to\mathbb R^k \]

be the Minkowski embedding

\[ \Phi(x)=(\tau_1(x),\dots,\tau_k(x)). \]

Set

\[ \Lambda=M^{-1}\mathcal O_K. \]

Since \(K\) is totally real and has root discriminant \(\Delta\),

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Phi(\mathcal O_K)) = \sqrt{|\operatorname{disc}(K)|} = \Delta^{k/2}. \]

Therefore

\[ \operatorname{covol}(\Phi(\Lambda)) = \left(\frac{\sqrt\Delta}{M}\right)^k. \]

For \(\rho>0\) and \(\mathbf t\in\mathbb R^k\), define

\[ B_\rho(\mathbf t) = \{x\in\Lambda:\Phi(x)\in \mathbf t+[-\rho,\rho]^k\}. \]

Averaging over a fundamental domain of \(\Phi(\Lambda)\),

\[ \mathbb E_{\mathbf t}|B_2(\mathbf t)| = \frac{\operatorname{vol}([-2,2]^k)} {\operatorname{covol}(\Phi(\Lambda))} = \frac{4^k}{(\sqrt\Delta/M)^k} = C_{\mathrm{in}}^k. \]

Hence there exists \(\mathbf t=\mathbf t_K\) such that

\[ |B_2(\mathbf t_K)|\ge C_{\mathrm{in}}^k. \]

Fix such a translate and write

\[ B_{\mathrm{in}}=B_2(\mathbf t_K), \qquad B_{\mathrm{out}}=B_3(\mathbf t_K). \]

Then

\[ |B_{\mathrm{in}}|\ge C_{\mathrm{in}}^k. \]

We next upper-bound \(B_{\mathrm{out}}\). If \(x,y\in\Lambda\) and \(x\ne y\), then

\[ M(x-y)\in\mathcal O_K\setminus\{0\}. \]

Thus

\[ \left|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(M(x-y))\right|\ge 1. \]

Equivalently,

\[ \prod_{j=1}^k |\tau_j(x-y)|\ge M^{-k}. \]

Therefore

\[ \max_j |\tau_j(x-y)|\ge M^{-1}. \]

So the points of \(\Phi(\Lambda)\) are separated in \(\ell^\infty\)-distance by at least \(M^{-1}\).

The \(\ell^\infty\)-cubes of radius \(1/(2M)\) centered at the points of \(\Phi(B_{\mathrm{out}})\) have disjoint interiors and all lie inside a cube of side length

\[ 6+\frac1M. \]

Each small cube has volume \(M^{-k}\). Therefore

\[ |B_{\mathrm{out}}|M^{-k} \le \left(6+\frac1M\right)^k, \]

so

\[ |B_{\mathrm{out}}| \le (6M+1)^k = C_{\mathrm{out}}^k. \]

Finally, if \(c\in M^{-1}\mathcal O_K\) satisfies

\[ |\tau_j(c)|\le 1 \]

for all \(j\), then

\[ B_{\mathrm{in}}+c\subseteq B_{\mathrm{out}}, \]

because the inner box has radius \(2\), the outer box has radius \(3\), and every conjugate of \(c\) has absolute value at most \(1\).

6. The planar point set

Use the first real embedding

\[ \sigma=\tau_1. \]

Define

\[ P_K = \{(\sigma(x),\sigma(y)):x,y\in B_{\mathrm{out}}\} \subset\mathbb R^2. \]

Since \(\sigma\) is injective on \(K\), the map

\[ B_{\mathrm{out}}\times B_{\mathrm{out}} \to P_K, \qquad (x,y)\mapsto(\sigma(x),\sigma(y)) \]

is injective. Hence

\[ |P_K|=|B_{\mathrm{out}}|^2. \]

Therefore

\[ |P_K|\le C_{\mathrm{out}}^{2k}. \]

Also \(B_{\mathrm{in}}\subseteq B_{\mathrm{out}}\), so

\[ |P_K|\ge |B_{\mathrm{in}}|^2\ge C_{\mathrm{in}}^{2k}. \]

Since \(C_{\mathrm{in}}>1\) and \(k=k_i\to\infty\), we will have

\[ |P_{K_i}|\to\infty. \]

7. Counting unit distances

Let \((a,b)\in\mathcal U_K\). Then

\[ a,b\in M^{-1}\mathcal O_K, \]

and for every embedding \(\tau_j\),

\[ |\tau_j(a)|\le 1,\qquad |\tau_j(b)|\le 1. \]

Thus

\[ B_{\mathrm{in}}+a\subseteq B_{\mathrm{out}}, \qquad B_{\mathrm{in}}+b\subseteq B_{\mathrm{out}}. \]

For every \(x,y\in B_{\mathrm{in}}\), define

\[ p=(\sigma(x),\sigma(y)), \qquad q=(\sigma(x+a),\sigma(y+b)). \]

Then \(p,q\in P_K\). Moreover,

\[ \|p-q\|_2^2 = \sigma(a)^2+\sigma(b)^2 = \sigma(a^2+b^2) = \sigma(1) = 1. \]

So \((p,q)\) is a directed unit-distance pair.

The pairs constructed in this way are all distinct. Indeed, if two quadruples \((a,b,x,y)\) and \((a',b',x',y')\) produce the same ordered pair \((p,q)\), then equality of the first coordinates gives

\[ \sigma(x)=\sigma(x'), \qquad \sigma(y)=\sigma(y'), \]

so \(x=x'\) and \(y=y'\). Equality of the second points then gives

\[ \sigma(a)=\sigma(a'), \qquad \sigma(b)=\sigma(b'), \]

so \(a=a'\) and \(b=b'\). Thus no overcounting occurs.

Therefore

\[ |D(P_K)| \ge |\mathcal U_K|\,|B_{\mathrm{in}}|^2 \ge A^k C_{\mathrm{in}}^{2k}. \]

Hence

\[ u(P_K) = \frac12 |D(P_K)| \ge \frac12 A^k C_{\mathrm{in}}^{2k}. \]

By definition of \(\Theta\),

\[ A C_{\mathrm{in}}^2 = \Theta C_{\mathrm{out}}^2. \]

Thus

\[ u(P_K) \ge \frac12 \Theta^k C_{\mathrm{out}}^{2k}. \]

On the other hand,

\[ |P_K|\le C_{\mathrm{out}}^{2k}. \]

Therefore

\[ |P_K|^{1+\delta} \le C_{\mathrm{out}}^{2k(1+\delta)} = C_{\mathrm{out}}^{2k} \left(C_{\mathrm{out}}^{2\delta}\right)^k. \]

Since

\[ \delta= \frac{\log\Theta}{4\log C_{\mathrm{out}}}, \]

we have

\[ C_{\mathrm{out}}^{2\delta} = \Theta^{1/2}. \]

Thus

\[ |P_K|^{1+\delta} \le C_{\mathrm{out}}^{2k}\Theta^{k/2}. \]

Because \(\Theta>4\), we have

\[ \frac12\Theta^k\ge \Theta^{k/2} \]

for every \(k\ge 1\). Hence

\[ u(P_K)\ge |P_K|^{1+\delta}. \]

Applying this construction to \(K=K_i\), define

\[ P_i=P_{K_i}. \]

Then

\[ |P_i|\to\infty \]

and

\[ u(P_i)\ge |P_i|^{1+\delta} \]

for all \(i\). This proves the theorem. \(\square\)
