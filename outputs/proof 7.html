Below I first point out the main gaps in the previous sketch, and then give a corrected proof.

The previous proof had three places that needed real justification:

  1. Regulators. I asserted too quickly that bounded root discriminant gives exponentially bounded regulators. One needs a standard Landau–Stark residue bound plus the analytic class number formula, and then a separate comparison between the ordinary regulator and the relative norm-one regulator.
  2. Relative units. I used the fact that the relative norm-one unit group has rank proportional to the degree. This is true, but it needs a signature computation and a finite-index argument.
  3. Sumsets. Exact sumsets \(kU\) are not literally nested as sets. They are only nondecreasing in cardinality because \(1\in U\), via the injection \(x\mapsto x+1\). The proof below uses only cardinality monotonicity.

We now give the complete proof.

1. Arithmetic input

We use the following classical theorem of Martinet.

Martinet tower theorem. There exists an infinite sequence of totally real number fields

\[ F_1,F_2,\dots \]

with \([F_i:\mathbb Q]\to\infty\), and with uniformly bounded root discriminants:

\[ |\operatorname{Disc}(F_i)|^{1/[F_i:\mathbb Q]}\le \Delta_F \]

for some absolute constant \(\Delta_F\).

Fix such a tower. Choose a totally real base field \(F_0\) inside the tower, and choose an algebraic integer \(\theta\in \mathcal O_{F_0}\) with mixed signs at the real embeddings of \(F_0\): positive at at least one real embedding and negative at at least one real embedding. Such a \(\theta\) exists by weak approximation.

For each \(i\), set

\[ L_i:=F_i(\sqrt{\theta}). \]

Let

\[ d_i=[L_i:\mathbb Q]. \]

Since \(\theta\) is negative at some real embedding of \(F_i\), it is not a square in \(F_i\), so

\[ [L_i:F_i]=2. \]

Let \(\rho_i\) be the nontrivial \(F_i\)-automorphism of \(L_i\), so

\[ \rho_i(\sqrt{\theta})=-\sqrt{\theta}. \]

Because \(\theta\) has both positive and negative real signs, \(L_i\) has both real and complex places.

Choose one real embedding of \(F_0\) at which \(\theta<0\). For every \(i\), choose a complex embedding

\[ \sigma_i:L_i\hookrightarrow \mathbb C \]

lying over an extension of that real embedding. Then

\[ \sigma_i\circ \rho_i=\overline{\sigma_i}. \]

2. Bounded root discriminants and relative regulators

Let

\[ n_i=[F_i:\mathbb Q]. \]

Then

\[ d_i=2n_i. \]

The relative discriminant of \(L_i/F_i\) divides the extension of the fixed ideal \((4\theta)\subseteq \mathcal O_{F_0}\). Hence

\[ |\operatorname{Disc}(L_i)|^{1/d_i} \]

is uniformly bounded. Indeed,

\[ \operatorname{Disc}(L_i) = \operatorname{Disc}(F_i)^2 N_{F_i/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak d_{L_i/F_i}), \]

and

\[ \mathfrak d_{L_i/F_i}\mid (4\theta)\mathcal O_{F_i}. \]

Therefore there is a constant \(\Delta_L\) such that

\[ |\operatorname{Disc}(L_i)|^{1/d_i}\le \Delta_L \]

for all \(i\).

We need the following standard consequence of the analytic class number formula.

Regulator bound. For every \(\Delta>0\), there exists \(C_\Delta>0\) such that if \(K\) is a number field of degree \(m\) and root discriminant at most \(\Delta\), then its ordinary regulator satisfies

\[ R_K\le C_\Delta^m. \]

Also, by Friedman’s lower bound for regulators, there exists \(c>0\) such that for every number field \(K\) of positive unit rank,

\[ R_K\ge c^{[K:\mathbb Q]}. \]

The upper bound follows from Landau’s residue estimate

\[ \operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s) \le \left(C\frac{\log |\operatorname{Disc}(K)|}{[K:\mathbb Q]}\right)^{[K:\mathbb Q]} \]

and the analytic class number formula.

Now define the relative norm-one unit group

\[ E_i^1 := \{u\in \mathcal O_{L_i}^{\times}:u\rho_i(u)=1\}. \]

Let \(s_i\) be the number of real places of \(F_i\) at which \(\theta>0\). Since \(\theta\) is positive at a fixed positive proportion of the real embeddings coming from \(F_0\), there is a constant \(\kappa>0\) such that

\[ s_i\ge \kappa d_i. \]

At each such split real place \(v\), choose one of the two real embeddings

\[ \tau_v:L_i\hookrightarrow \mathbb R \]

above \(v\). Define

\[ \ell_i:E_i^1\to \mathbb R^{s_i} \]

by

\[ \ell_i(u)=\bigl(\log|\tau_v(u)|\bigr)_v. \]

The image \(\Lambda_i:=\ell_i(E_i^1)\) is a full-rank lattice in \(\mathbb R^{s_i}\). Its rank is \(s_i\). Indeed, \(L_i\) has signature

\[ (r_1,r_2)=(2s_i,n_i-s_i), \]

so

\[ \operatorname{rank}\mathcal O_{L_i}^{\times} = r_1+r_2-1 = n_i+s_i-1, \]

whereas

\[ \operatorname{rank}\mathcal O_{F_i}^{\times} = n_i-1. \]

The quotient of \(\mathcal O_{L_i}^{\times}\) by the subgroup generated by \(\mathcal O_{F_i}^{\times}\) and \(E_i^1\) is killed by \(2\), because for every unit \(u\),

\[ u^2 = N_{L_i/F_i}(u)\cdot \frac{u}{\rho_i(u)}, \]

with

\[ N_{L_i/F_i}(u)\in \mathcal O_{F_i}^{\times}, \qquad \frac{u}{\rho_i(u)}\in E_i^1. \]

Thus \(E_i^1\) has rank \(s_i\).

Let

\[ R_i^1:=\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_i). \]

We claim that

\[ R_i^1\le R_0^{d_i} \]

for some constant \(R_0\) independent of \(i\).

To see this, compare the full logarithmic lattice of \(\mathcal O_{L_i}^{\times}\) with the direct sum of the logarithmic lattice of \(\mathcal O_{F_i}^{\times}\) and that of \(E_i^1\). In the full logarithmic embedding, the base-field units contribute vectors of the form \((x,x)\) at split real places, while norm-one units contribute vectors of the form \((y,-y)\). These subspaces are orthogonal. The finite-index argument above gives index at most \(2^{O(d_i)}\). Therefore

\[ R_i^1 \le C^{d_i}\frac{R_{L_i}}{R_{F_i}}. \]

Using the upper bound \(R_{L_i}\le C_1^{d_i}\) and the lower bound \(R_{F_i}\ge C_2^{-d_i}\), we obtain

\[ R_i^1\le R_0^{d_i}. \]

3. Many bounded norm-one units

Fix \(T>1\), to be chosen later. Consider the box

\[ [-T,T]^{s_i}\subset \mathbb R^{s_i}. \]

By Blichfeldt’s theorem, applied to the lattice \(\Lambda_i\), we have

\[ |\Lambda_i\cap [-T,T]^{s_i}| \ge \frac12\frac{T^{s_i}}{R_i^1} \]

for all sufficiently large \(i\). Hence

\[ |\Lambda_i\cap [-T,T]^{s_i}| \ge \frac12 \left(\frac{T^\kappa}{R_0}\right)^{d_i}. \]

Choose \(T\) large enough so that

\[ \frac{T^\kappa}{R_0}>4. \]

Then there exists \(\alpha>1\) such that, for all large \(i\),

\[ |\Lambda_i\cap [-T,T]^{s_i}| \ge \alpha^{d_i}. \]

Define

\[ U_i := \{u\in E_i^1:\|\ell_i(u)\|_\infty\le T\}. \]

Then

\[ |U_i|\ge \alpha^{d_i}. \]

Let

\[ M:=e^T. \]

For every \(u\in U_i\), we have

\[ |\sigma_i(u)|=1, \]

because

\[ \sigma_i(u)\overline{\sigma_i(u)} = \sigma_i(u)\sigma_i(\rho_i(u)) = \sigma_i(u\rho_i(u)) = 1. \]

Moreover, for every embedding \(\tau:L_i\hookrightarrow \mathbb C\),

\[ |\tau(u)|\le M. \]

Indeed, at split real places this follows from \(\|\ell_i(u)\|_\infty\le T\), and at complex places above negative real places the norm-one condition gives modulus \(1\).

Thus we have produced, for each large \(i\), a set \(U_i\) of algebraic units satisfying:

  1. \(|U_i|\ge \alpha^{d_i}\);
  2. \(|\sigma_i(u)|=1\) for all \(u\in U_i\);
  3. \(|\tau(u)|\le M\) for every embedding \(\tau\).

Also \(1\in U_i\).

4. A packing bound for sumsets

For a positive integer \(k\), define

\[ kU_i = \{u_1+\cdots+u_k:u_1,\dots,u_k\in U_i\}. \]

We prove:

Lemma. There is an absolute constant \(C_{\mathrm{pack}}\) such that, for all \(i\) and all \(k\ge 1\),

\[ |kU_i|\le (C_{\mathrm{pack}}kM)^{d_i}. \]

Proof. Let \(x,y\in kU_i\) be distinct. Then

\[ z:=x-y \]

is a nonzero algebraic integer. Therefore

\[ |N_{L_i/\mathbb Q}(z)|\ge 1. \]

Let

\[ r:=|\sigma_i(z)|. \]

The conjugate embedding \(\overline{\sigma_i}\) contributes the same absolute value \(r\). For every other embedding \(\tau\),

\[ |\tau(z)| \le |\tau(x)|+|\tau(y)| \le 2kM. \]

Thus

\[ 1 \le |N_{L_i/\mathbb Q}(z)| \le r^2(2kM)^{d_i-2}. \]

Hence

\[ r\ge (2kM)^{-(d_i-2)/2}. \]

So distinct points of \(\sigma_i(kU_i)\subset \mathbb C\) are separated by at least

\[ \rho:=(2kM)^{-(d_i-2)/2}. \]

On the other hand, since \(|\sigma_i(u)|=1\) for all \(u\in U_i\), every point of \(\sigma_i(kU_i)\) lies in the disk of radius \(k\). A planar packing bound gives

\[ |kU_i| = |\sigma_i(kU_i)| \le \left(\frac{5k}{\rho}\right)^2 \le (C_{\mathrm{pack}}kM)^{d_i} \]

for an absolute constant \(C_{\mathrm{pack}}\). ∎

5. Choosing a sumset with small growth

Fix a large integer \(K\), to be chosen momentarily, and set

\[ B:=C_{\mathrm{pack}}KM. \]

Then

\[ |KU_i|\le B^{d_i}. \]

Since

\[ |U_i|\ge \alpha^{d_i}, \]

we have

\[ \frac{|KU_i|}{|U_i|} \le \left(\frac{B}{\alpha}\right)^{d_i}. \]

Because \(1\in U_i\), the map

\[ x\mapsto x+1 \]

is an injection \(jU_i\hookrightarrow (j+1)U_i\). Hence the cardinalities \(|jU_i|\) are nondecreasing, and

\[ \prod_{j=2}^{K} \frac{|jU_i|}{|(j-1)U_i|} = \frac{|KU_i|}{|U_i|} \le \left(\frac{B}{\alpha}\right)^{d_i}. \]

Therefore there exists some \(j_i\in\{2,\dots,K\}\) such that

\[ \frac{|j_iU_i|}{|(j_i-1)U_i|} \le \exp(\varepsilon d_i), \]

where

\[ \varepsilon := \frac{\log(B/\alpha)}{K-1}. \]

Since

\[ B=C_{\mathrm{pack}}KM, \]

we have

\[ \varepsilon = \frac{\log K+O(1)}{K-1}\to 0 \]

as \(K\to\infty\). Choose \(K\) so large that

\[ \varepsilon<\frac12\log\alpha. \]

Define

\[ \lambda:=\log\alpha-\varepsilon>0. \]

6. Constructing the point set

For each sufficiently large \(i\), put

\[ A_i:=(j_i-1)U_i, \qquad B_i:=j_iU_i. \]

Define the point set

\[ P_i:=\sigma_i(A_i\cup B_i)\subset \mathbb C\cong \mathbb R^2. \]

Let

\[ n_i:=|P_i|. \]

We first bound \(n_i\). Since

\[ |B_i|\le e^{\varepsilon d_i}|A_i|, \]

we have

\[ n_i \le |A_i|+|B_i| \le 2e^{\varepsilon d_i}|A_i|. \]

Also, because \(j_i\le K\) and \(1\in U_i\), both \(|A_i|\) and \(|B_i|\) are at most \(|KU_i|\). Indeed, one pads sums with copies of \(1\). Therefore

\[ n_i \le 2|KU_i| \le 2B^{d_i}. \]

Now count unit distances. For every pair

\[ (a,u)\in A_i\times U_i, \]

we have

\[ a+u\in B_i. \]

Therefore both

\[ \sigma_i(a) \quad\text{and}\quad \sigma_i(a+u) \]

belong to \(P_i\), and

\[ |\sigma_i(a+u)-\sigma_i(a)| = |\sigma_i(u)| = 1. \]

The map

\[ (a,u) \mapsto \bigl(\sigma_i(a),\sigma_i(a+u)\bigr) \]

is injective. Indeed, if two such ordered pairs coincide, then \(\sigma_i(a)=\sigma_i(a')\), hence \(a=a'\), and then \(\sigma_i(u)=\sigma_i(u')\), hence \(u=u'\).

Thus we obtain at least \(|A_i||U_i|\) ordered unit-distance pairs. Therefore

\[ u(P_i) \ge \frac12 |A_i||U_i|. \]

Using

\[ |U_i|\ge \alpha^{d_i} \]

and

\[ |A_i| \ge \frac{n_i}{2e^{\varepsilon d_i}}, \]

we get

\[ u(P_i) \ge \frac14 n_i e^{-\varepsilon d_i}\alpha^{d_i} = \frac14 n_i e^{\lambda d_i}. \]

Since

\[ n_i\le 2B^{d_i}, \]

we have

\[ e^{\lambda d_i} \ge \left(\frac{n_i}{2}\right)^{\lambda/\log B}. \]

Hence

\[ u(P_i) \ge c\, n_i^{1+\delta_0}, \]

where

\[ \delta_0:=\frac{\lambda}{\log B}>0 \]

and \(c>0\) is an absolute constant.

Finally, since \(1\in U_i\), we have

\[ |A_i|\ge |U_i|\ge \alpha^{d_i}, \]

so

\[ n_i\to\infty. \]

Discarding finitely many initial terms, we may absorb the constant \(c\) into the exponent. Namely, for all sufficiently large \(i\),

\[ c\, n_i^{1+\delta_0} \ge n_i^{1+\delta_0/2}. \]

Set

\[ \delta:=\frac{\delta_0}{2}>0. \]

Then, after relabeling the remaining infinite sequence,

\[ u(P_i)\ge |P_i|^{1+\delta} \]

for all \(i\), with \(|P_i|\to\infty\).

This proves the theorem. ∎
